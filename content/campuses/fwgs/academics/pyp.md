## What is the IB Primary Years Programme?

The IB Primary Years Programme (PYP) is a leading curriculum framework for international primary education. At Fountainhead Wockhardt Global School (FWGS), the PYP is offered to students from Nursery to PYP 5 (Grade 5).

The PYP focuses on the development of the **whole child as an inquirer**, both in the classroom and in the world outside. It is not simply a subject-based programme — it is a philosophy of education that teaches children *how to learn*, *how to think*, and *how to act* in an interconnected world.

## 10 Reasons the PYP is Ideal for Your Child

1. **It encourages students to inquire** — the PYP fosters natural curiosity and learning in creative, supportive, and collaborative environments
2. **Understanding a complex and interdependent world** — PYP students create meaning for themselves and build understanding by exploring real-world issues
3. **PYP students are confident communicators** — they learn to communicate in a variety of ways and in more than one language
4. **Learning how to learn** — PYP students work collaboratively with teachers and other students to plan, present, and assess their own learning
5. **It encourages international-mindedness** — collaboration and understanding of their own and other cultures are an important focus; students learn how to be respectful and open-minded
6. **Seeing things from different perspectives** — PYP students use critical and creative thinking to develop knowledge, understanding, and skills within and across subject areas
7. **Students take action** — PYP students believe they are able to grow and succeed; they make appropriate choices and take responsibility for their actions
8. **Thinking about issues** — creative learning gives students the agility and imagination to respond to new and unexpected challenges and opportunities in an increasingly globalised and uncertain world
9. **It involves the whole school learning community** — together we celebrate our common humanity and the belief that education can help to build a better and more peaceful world
10. **Caring and responsible citizens** — students can express ideas and opinions, and they can propose solutions that make a difference in their lives and the lives of others

## The PYP Curriculum Framework

The PYP curriculum places the **IB Learner Profile** at the centre, surrounded by:

- **6 Transdisciplinary Themes** (the units of inquiry)
- **Subject areas**: Language, Social Studies, Mathematics, Science, Arts, Physical, Social and Personal Education
- **Agency**: Voice, Choice, and Ownership — students have a say in their learning
- **Approaches to Teaching and Learning (ATL)**
- **Action**: meaningful student-initiated action in response to learning
- **Exhibition**: the culminating PYP event in Grade 5

## Transdisciplinary Themes

Learning at FWGS PYP is organised around six internationally significant themes that connect subjects and connect learning to the real world:

| Theme | What students inquire into |
|---|---|
| **Who We Are** | Identity as individuals and as part of a collective — physical, emotional, and spiritual health and well-being; relationships and belonging; learning and growing |
| **Where We Are in Place and Time** | Histories and orientation in place, space, and time — periods, events, and artifacts; communities, heritage, culture, and environment; natural and human drivers of movement, adaptation, and transformation |
| **How We Express Ourselves** | The diversity of voice, perspectives, and expression — inspiration, imagination, creativity; personal, social, and cultural notes and practices of communication; intentions, perceptions, interpretations, and responses |
| **How the World Works** | Understandings of the world and phenomena — patterns, cycles, systems; diverse practices, methods, and tools; approaches to livelihoods and trade practices; intended and unintended consequences |
| **How We Organise Ourselves** | Systems, structures, and networks — interactions with and between social and ecological systems; approaches to livelihoods and trade practices; representation, collaboration, and decision-making |
| **Sharing the Planet** | Interdependence of human and natural worlds — rights, responsibilities, and dignity of all; pathways to just, peaceful, and reimagined futures; nature, complexity, coexistence, and wisdom |

## Key Concepts in the PYP

Specified concepts help to frame units of inquiry and drive learning experiences. The seven key concepts are:

- **Form** — What is it like? (Everything has a form with recognisable features that can be observed, identified, described, and categorised)
- **Function** — How does it work? (Everything has a purpose, a role, or a way of behaving that can be investigated)
- **Causation** — Why is it as it is? (Things do not just happen; there are causal relationships at work, and actions have consequences)
- **Change** — How is it transforming? (Change is the process of movement from one state to another; it is universal and inevitable)
- **Connection** — How is it linked to other things? (We live in a world of interacting systems; the actions of any individual element affect others)
- **Perspective** — What are the points of view? (Knowledge is moderated by different points of view which lead to different interpretations, understandings, and findings)
- **Responsibility** — What are our obligations? (People make choices based on their understandings, beliefs, and values; the actions they take as a result do make a difference)

## Approaches to Learning (ATL Skills)

The PYP develops five essential skill categories in every student:

- **Social Skills** — positive interpersonal relationships, collaboration, social-emotional intelligence
- **Research Skills** — information literacy, media literacy, ethical use of information
- **Thinking Skills** — critical thinking, creative thinking, transfer skills, reflection and metacognition
- **Communication Skills** — exchanging information, literacy, ICT skills
- **Self-Management Skills** — organisation skills, states of mind

## Agency in the PYP

At the heart of the PYP is a commitment to **learner agency** — built on three dimensions:

- **Voice** — students express their perspectives and advocate for their learning
- **Choice** — students make meaningful decisions about their learning
- **Ownership** — students take responsibility for their learning journey

Agency is supported by a **trusted learning community** that is timeless, transformational, and future-building.

## Approaches to Teaching

At FWGS, PYP teaching is:

- Based on inquiry
- Focused on conceptual understanding
- Developed in local and global contexts
- Focused on effective teamwork and collaboration
- Differentiated to meet the needs of all learners
- Informed by formative and summative assessment

## Assessment in the PYP

Assessment in the PYP involves four interconnected processes:

| Process | What it involves |
|---|---|
| **Monitoring** | Daily observation, questioning, reflecting, discussing, learning with peers and teachers, feedback to feed forward |
| **Documenting** | Compilation of evidence; making learning visible or digital; displayed or recorded in a variety of media |
| **Measuring** | Captures a point-in-time view of learning |
| **Reporting** | Describes progress; attainment of students' learning; identifies areas for growth |

## The FWGS Learner Profile

Based on the IB Learner Profile, FWGS students are nurtured to become:

**Inquirers · Knowledgeable · Thinkers · Communicators · Balanced · Open-minded · Caring · Courageous · Principled · Reflective · Resilient**

These attributes guide everything — from classroom behaviour to how students engage with the world beyond school.

## Action in the PYP

The PYP believes that learning must lead to meaningful action. Students are encouraged to act in response to their learning in ways that include:

- **Participation** — contributing as an individual or group
- **Advocacy** — action to support social, environmental, or political change
- **Social Justice** — relation to rights, equality, equity, social well-being, and justice
- **Social Entrepreneurship** — innovative, resourceful, and sustainable social change
- **Lifestyle Choices** — e.g., consumption, impact of choices on the environment
