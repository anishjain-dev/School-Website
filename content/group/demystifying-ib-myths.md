The IB has a reputation that precedes it — sometimes accurately, often not. These are the questions and concerns we hear most often from parents considering Fountainhead Schools, answered plainly.

---

**"The IB is only for gifted children."**

False. The IB was designed for all learners. The Primary Years Programme (PYP) and Middle Years Programme (MYP) are not selective — they are frameworks for every child to develop curiosity, critical thinking and agency. The Diploma Programme (DP) is challenging, but it is challenging in a way that prepares students for university and life — not just for one exam.

At Fountainhead, we have seen students of all starting points thrive in the IB framework because it respects how children actually learn, rather than asking them to memorise and reproduce.

---

**"The IB is too expensive because it doesn't focus on grades."**

The IB absolutely assesses students. Every programme has formal assessment — internal and external. What is different is that assessment in the IB is designed to measure genuine understanding, not recall under pressure. A student who understands the material will also perform well on assessments.

---

**"IB students can't get into Indian universities."**

IB Diploma students are accepted by every major Indian university, including IITs and NITs (through the DASA/CIWG scheme), DU, Ashoka, Flame, NIAS, and all other central and state universities. Most Indian universities have updated their IB equivalency policies. The DP's depth in Mathematics, Sciences and Languages prepares students well for Indian university entrance.

---

**"The IB doesn't teach enough content — it's all projects and discussion."**

The IB curriculum is rigorous in content. The Diploma Programme, for example, requires students to study six subjects in depth alongside Theory of Knowledge, an Extended Essay and the CAS programme. The content is equivalent to — and in many cases deeper than — national board syllabi. The difference is in the method: students learn to engage with content, not just absorb it.

---

**"You have to be fluent in English to do the IB."**

The IB promotes multilingualism. Students are required to study a mother tongue language alongside English. At Fountainhead, we actively support students in maintaining and developing their first language. The IB's language policy is one of its strongest features — it sees every child's home language as an asset, not an obstacle.

---

**"IB grades don't compare to CBSE or ICSE — universities don't know how to read them."**

The IB Diploma is recognised by over 5,000 universities in 150+ countries. In India, the Association of Indian Universities (AIU) has issued equivalency certificates for the IB Diploma since the 1990s. University admissions teams — Indian and international — understand the IB grade scale well.

---

**"The IB is very stressful for students."**

Any rigorous academic programme involves effort. The difference with the IB is that the stress, when it appears, is usually productive — it comes from genuine intellectual challenge, not arbitrary exam pressure. The IB's design distributes assessment over time, requires students to develop independent study skills, and builds in reflection. Students who go through the IB typically report that it prepared them exceptionally well for the demands of university.

---

**"Fountainhead is only for students who want to go abroad."**

Many of our graduates go to Indian universities and go on to build careers, businesses and lives in India. The IB's skills — inquiry, communication, international-mindedness, research — are valuable everywhere. The school is not preparing students for one geography or one career type. It is preparing them to navigate whatever comes next.
