Fountainhead runs the full IB continuum. A child can move from their first years of school through to a university-entrance qualification without changing the way they are taught — the same conviction about how children learn, carried across three programmes.

## The FS Learning Model

With students at the centre of everything we do, at Fountainhead we place a strong emphasis on fostering a love of learning. Our pedagogy is learner-centred and aims to create a positive and supportive environment. We believe that learning needs to be goal-directed and with that in mind, we offer experiential learning opportunities through innovative curricular practices and teaching methods. We help students focus on academic competencies, but at the same time understand the value of physical fitness, creativity and social-emotional well-being. The values we hope to inculcate in our students seek to develop the skills, attitudes, and character necessary for long-term success in our students.

## The Role of Parents in Education

At Fountainhead, parents are partners — not spectators. The school believes that a child's education is best when the school and the parents are in genuine partnership.

**What we ask of parents:**

- Attend Parent Orientation sessions, Parent-Teacher Conferences and Student-Led Conferences
- Participate in exhibitions, role plays, sports days and school events
- Support your child in self-study and homework — but not do it for them
- Build a culture of reading and independent thinking at home
- Engage authentically with your child about school life — through conversation, not interrogation
- Help with surveys, projects and research by asking the right questions, not by doing it for them

**At home:**

- Build a relationship based on mutual trust and respect rather than fear or force
- Encourage independence in work, learning habits and daily routines
- Expose children to a wide variety of places and experiences beyond the standard malls and restaurants
- Model family values such as integrity, commitment, respect and tolerance
- Maintain balanced routines that support physical, mental and socio-emotional development

The consequences of not playing this role are natural ones — experienced by the child and family, not enforced by the school.

## The continuum

- **Primary Years Programme** — transdisciplinary inquiry, ages 3–12.
- **Middle Years Programme** — concept-driven and interdisciplinary, ages 11–16.
- **Diploma Programme** — six subject groups plus the core, ages 16–19.

Each programme has its own page with the detail that matters to a parent
deciding: age range, how learning is structured, and how it is assessed.
