## Objective

To address disciplinary issues related to bullying in school. The aim is to develop self-discipline in students to take responsibility for their own actions, while ensuring that consequences are just, fair and appropriate.

## What is Bullying?

Bullying is intentionally aggressive behaviour that can take many forms — verbal, physical, social/relational/emotional, or cyber — or any combination of these. It involves an imbalance of power and is often repeated over a period of time.

Bullying can consist of one student targeting another, a group ganging up against one student, or one group targeting another group. It is generally unprovoked.

**Bully:** A person who threatens, hurts or scares others to get what they want.

**Victim:** The person being bullied.

**Bystander:** People who watch bullying happen or hear about it but do not play a major role in preventing it.

## Types of Bullying

![The 6 Types of Bullying](/bullying-types.png)

- **Verbal** — name calling, teasing, threatening language
- **Physical** — hitting, pushing, kicking, taking belongings
- **Social / Relational** — excluding someone, spreading rumours, damaging friendships
- **Cyber** — bullying through digital devices, social media or messages

## Possible Signs of Bullying

A student may have been bullied if they show some of the following signs. Adults should investigate if a student:

- Does not want to get on the school bus or begs to be driven to school
- Changes their usual routine
- Becomes withdrawn, anxious, or lacking in confidence
- Starts stammering or attempts or threatens to run away
- Cries at night, has nightmares or bed-wetting
- Feels ill in the morning or begins to do poorly in school work
- Comes home with torn clothes or damaged books
- Has possessions that go missing, or asks for money or starts stealing (to pay the bully)
- Has unexplained cuts or bruises, or comes home hungry (money or lunch was stolen)
- Becomes aggressive, disruptive or unreasonable, or starts bullying siblings
- Stops eating or is frightened to say what is wrong
- Gives improbable excuses for any of the above

These signs could indicate other problems, but bullying should always be investigated as a possibility.

## Why Do People Bully?

- To avoid facing their own inadequacy
- Because they believe they are superior to others
- To obtain something they cannot achieve on their own
- To reduce their fear of being seen as weak or incompetent
- To attract attention from others

## Who Do Bullies Target?

- People who do not stand up for themselves
- People who are quiet and unlikely to tell adults
- People who are isolated from peers or adults
- People who get upset easily or cry

## What Can You Do if Someone is Bullying You?

- Tell someone you trust or approach a school authority — it is not easy to solve this alone
- Stay away from the bully as much as you can
- If a bully tries to hurt you, stand up for yourself, but try not to fight — walk away or yell to get help

## What Should You Do if You Are Bullying Others?

Hurting other people does not make you important; it makes you mean.

- Think about ways to be a leader without hurting others — get involved in sports, school groups or community activities
- If you need advice, talk to someone you trust
- Remember: treat others the way you would like to be treated

## What Should You Do if You See Bullying?

- Go to the person being bullied and lead them away without engaging with the bully
- Do not make the bully feel important by paying attention to them
- Most bullying ends if someone steps in to stop it

## What to Do with the Bully and the Victim

Minor incidents may be handled in the classroom by the class teacher. If bullying persists or a serious incident occurs, it must be reported to the Head of School.

- The victim is sent to the school counsellor
- The bully is dealt with by the school authority
- Disciplinary measures are taken based on the severity of the incident

## Consequences of Bullying

Bullying causes lasting harm — to those being bullied, those who bully, and those who witness it. It can lead to self-doubt, low confidence, anxiety, depression and, in serious cases, may affect mental health significantly.

## Steps Taken by the School

This policy is implemented when students are found:

1. Using abusive or derogatory words towards peers, teachers, support staff or any other member of the school community
2. Hitting, pushing or shoving another student in the classroom, on the stairs, on the playground, or any dangerous place
3. Bullying a lone student — from the same grade or another grade

**Terms used in this process:**

- **Complainer** — The student, didi or teacher who reports the incident
- **Faultier** — The student who displayed bullying behaviour
- **Arbiter** — The school authority (counsellor or Head of School) who handles the case

### Process

1. The incident must be reported to the Arbiter immediately. Reports older than 2 working days will not be considered.
2. The Homeroom Teacher records the incident in the Behavioural Observation Form.
3. A joint meeting is held between the complainer, faultier and arbiter to assess the incident and identify any witnesses.
4. If clear evidence is found against the faultier, consequences follow. If no evidence is found, both the faultier and complainer face consequences as per the Discipline Policy.

### Classroom Prevention

- Display anti-bullying rules in each classroom and on notice boards
- Talk and train students on bullying and how to respond
- Conduct regular follow-up sessions during Circle Time
- Have students sign an anti-bullying class pledge
- Do activities that promote class unity
- Provide an anonymous "notes to the teacher" box
- Take every student complaint seriously
- Get students and parents involved in anti-bullying awareness

## Consequences

Consequences follow the Order of Consequence as stated in the Discipline Policy.
