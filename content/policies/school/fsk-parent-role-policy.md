## Objective

To increase parents' roles and responsibilities as partners in their child's education and participation in meeting the educational needs of children.

## Rationale

The school believes that a child's education is best when the school and the parents are in genuine partnership. Parents must arrange appropriate learning environments at home to enable children to learn — without this, children cannot perform to their potential. Playing this role responsibly is a shared obligation.

## What We Expect from Parents

### At School

- Attend all Parent Orientation sessions to stay informed about what is new in the upcoming grade
- Attend all Parent-Teacher Conferences (PTCs) and Student-Led Conferences (SLCs) — these are the key occasions to understand your child's overall progress
- Participate in class presentations, role plays, exhibitions, sports day, annual day and other school events
- Volunteer for school events or activities such as arranging field trips, excursions and camps
- Follow school rules and regulations in intent and spirit
- Support your child in self-study and homework — but do not do the homework for them
- Take an active interest in your child's school activities through genuine conversation, not interrogation
- Understand which topics are being taught and help with surveys, projects and research by asking the right questions — not by outsourcing the work
- Support your child during student-led projects such as the PYP Exhibition, Community Project and Personal Projects

### At Home

- Build a culture of reading: read aloud to them, encourage them to read, make good books available, help them find reading buddies or a book club
- Encourage your child to talk and express themselves in their own time and in their own way
- Encourage independence in work, learning habits and daily routines
- Build a relationship based on mutual trust and respect rather than fear and force
- Get to know your child's friends — inside and outside school
- Expose your child to a wide variety of experiences and places, beyond the standard malls, restaurants and cinema halls
- Give exposure to a variety of activities while also building talent in one or two areas if possible
- Have reasonable expectations — do not expect your child to fulfil all your unrealised dreams; avoid over-scheduling free time, which leads to stress
- Allow time for free, unstructured play and relaxation — these are as important as structured activities
- Model and discuss universal human values: integrity, commitment, respect and tolerance
- Maintain balanced routines that include physical, mental and socio-emotional development

## Consequences

There are no enforced consequences for lack of parental role. The consequences are natural ones experienced by the child and family.
