## Connection to the School Mission

**"To nurture leaders with character and competence"** — Academic integrity is the commitment and demonstration of ethical behaviour in an academic setting. It gives every member of the Fountainhead community the opportunity to develop character — one of the core goals of the mission statement.

## Purpose

In an age where information is readily available and exponentially growing, academic integrity is about making knowledge, understanding, and thinking transparent. Students must understand how knowledge is constructed and their own role in furthering that construction. The technical skills — accurate citing and referencing — are essential, but the understanding of the concepts and values behind them comes first.

**The school's objectives:**

- Ensure all students understand what academic integrity means
- Prepare the school community for the responsible use of AI tools
- Show respect for the work of others
- Develop respect for the integrity of all forms of assessment
- Provide guidelines on study skills and academic writing
- Guide students on how to conduct research and acknowledge sources
- Make students understand what constitutes malpractice and its consequences
- Foster a school culture that actively encourages academic integrity
- Inform legal guardians of the policy through Parent Teacher Conferences and policy sessions

## What is Academic Integrity?

Academic Integrity refers to:

- A set of values — **honesty, trust, fairness, respect, and responsibility** — and skills that promote good practice in teaching, learning, and assessment
- Display of respect for and protection of intellectual property in all forms: creative expression, patents, registered designs, trademarks, moral rights, and copyright
- Promoting the use of students' own language, expression, and ideas; and appropriate acknowledgement when the ideas or work of another person are represented

## What to Cite

As per IB guidelines, any materials or ideas that are not one's own must be acknowledged — whether used through quotation, paraphrase, or summary. This includes:

Quotations · Visual materials · Pieces of music · Graphics · Artworks · Lectures · Interviews · Advertisements · Summaries · Photographs · Letters · Broadcasts · Maps · Charts · Tweets · Blogs · Information from websites or AI tools · Data from tables or graphs · Film scenes · Newspaper articles

Basic and common knowledge within a field does not need to be cited. When in doubt, cite.

## Key Terms

**School maladministration:** An action by an IB World School or an individual associated with it that infringes IB rules and regulations and potentially threatens the integrity of IB examinations and assessments.

**Academic misconduct:** Behaviour (deliberate or inadvertent) that results in, or may result in, a candidate gaining an unfair advantage in one or more components of assessment.

- **Plagiarism** — representing the ideas, words, or work of another person without proper acknowledgement
- **Collusion** — supporting malpractice by another candidate, such as allowing one's work to be copied or submitted for assessment by another
- **Duplication of work** — presenting the same work for different assessment components or IB diploma requirements
- **Misconduct during an IB examination** — taking unauthorised material into an examination; behaviour that disrupts or distracts other candidates; communicating with another candidate
- **Unethical behaviour** — inclusion of inappropriate material in assessment materials or breach of ethical guidelines during research

## Academic Integrity by Programme

### Primary Years Programme (PYP — Grades 1–6)

The focus in the Primary Years is on developing personal integrity and an understanding of why we acknowledge others' ideas.

**Early Years:** Accept each other's ideas and acknowledge the ideas of peers, parents, and teachers. Encourage students to develop personal integrity in group work and homework.

**Grades 1–6:** For culminating projects, cite the primary source for any ideas or information; cite all secondary sources (blogs, websites, video links). For group work, acknowledge all ideas shared by peers and guidance from teachers. For oral presentations, mention primary and secondary sources. For creative work, acknowledge ideas from parents, siblings, teachers, or experts; cite the secondary source or the artist's name.

**Teacher responsibilities (PYP):**
- Ensure students understand the expectations and guidelines
- Ensure students understand what constitutes academic misconduct
- Provide timely feedback and ensure appropriately referenced work

**Student responsibilities (PYP):**
- Be aware of the Academic Integrity policy
- Complete all assignments honestly and cite referred sources
- Show responsible use of the internet during research

**Parent responsibilities (PYP):**
- Understand the expectations of Academic Integrity
- Support school procedures that safeguard the authenticity of children's work
- Encourage students to add bibliography when working on assignments

### Middle Years Programme (MYP — Grades 7–10)

MLA 7 citation is mandatory for all IB and school submissions. In-text citation is compulsory.

**For culminating projects** (Personal Project, Community Project): cite sources using MLA 7; add in-text citation at the end of each paragraph or sentence where an expert's idea is used; add bibliography at the end of the report; take consent from participants during research; fill the academic honesty form; document and record the process in process journals.

**For group work:** acknowledge each member's contribution; cite sources using MLA 7.

**For oral presentations:** acknowledge primary and secondary sources; add bibliography at the end of the presentation.

**For creative work:** acknowledge original artwork; acknowledge ideas of others.

**Use of AI tools (2023 update):** If a student uses any text or product produced by an AI tool — by copying, paraphrasing, or modifying — they must clearly reference the AI tool in the body of their work and add it to the bibliography. The in-text citation should include the prompt given to the AI tool and the date the AI generated the text.

**Using software to improve language:** Software spell checkers and bilingual dictionaries are acceptable for most subjects. In Language Acquisition, where marks are awarded for sentence structure, such tools are not permitted. Students may not write essays in one language and then translate them for IB submission.

**Teacher responsibilities (MYP):**
- Ensure students have full understanding of the expectations for each subject
- Plan manageable workloads so students can produce work within IB expectations
- Ensure all student work is appropriately labelled and saved for submission
- Follow a cross-referencing plan for group projects to prevent collusion
- Report academic misconduct and support the school's and IB's investigations

**Student responsibilities (MYP):**
- Be aware of the Academic Integrity policy and programme guidelines
- Complete all assignments, tasks, examinations, and quizzes honestly
- Give credit for all sources including AI tools in all submitted work
- Abstain from receiving non-permitted assistance in completing or editing work
- Not use essay-writing or copy-editing services, pre-written essay banks, or file-sharing websites

**Parent/guardian responsibilities (MYP):**
- Understand IB policies, procedures, and subject guidelines
- Support children in planning manageable workloads
- Understand what constitutes misconduct and its consequences
- Abstain from giving or obtaining assistance in the completion of their child's work

### Diploma Programme (DP — Grades 11–12)

**For culminating projects** (Extended Essay, TOK essay, English HL essay, Internal Assessments): cite sources using MLA 7; add in-text citations; add bibliography at the end of each document; check plagiarism rate using Turnitin and work on the Turnitin report; submit through Google Docs/Slides so revision history is available.

**For group work** (Psychology Internal Assessment, Group 4 Project, CAS project): acknowledge each member's contribution; individual reports to be written.

**For oral presentations** (TOK exhibition, Global Politics, English A IO, Hindi B IO, French ab initio IO): verbal and written acknowledgements; bibliography at end of presentation; image links on respective slides.

**For creative work** (Visual Arts, Film, Design Technology): acknowledge original artwork; acknowledge ideas of others.

## Examination Integrity

### School Term Assessments

- No additional time is granted without school authorisation; access arrangements are communicated to invigilators in advance
- Subject teachers are not invigilators for the subject being examined
- Subject teachers may not enter the examination hall to assist their students
- Desks are checked; formula booklets are only allowed for specified papers
- Exam material is stored securely

### Final IB Examinations

- All IB exam material is stored in a locked cupboard in the strong room ("The Vault") — accessible only to the Head of School, Senior School Principal, Programme Coordinator, and Exam Officer
- No academic or admin staff may enter The Vault without permission
- Invigilators follow all processes set by IB as published in the yearly MYP Assessment Procedures
- Exam checklists are used to ensure integrity throughout the final IB exam process

## Consequences for Breach of Academic Integrity

**First instance:** A written warning is sent through student observation on Nucleus (to the student and parents). For missing citations in IB submissions, the student must complete or rewrite citations. The work is not marked but reported as "Academic Misconduct."

**Second repeated instance:** Parents are called in; the matter is discussed with the Programme Coordinator, Team Leader, parents, and student. The student is strictly informed that repetition will lead to suspension. The student rewrites the task under supervision.

**Third repeated instance:** The school has the right to suspend the student for up to seven days. The decision is made by the Programme Coordinator, Senior School Principal, and Head of School. In case of many repeated instances, the school may expel the student or mark "N" (non-submission) for the specific subject.

## Academic Integrity Practices for Staff

**Do:**
- Look up freely and legally available resources (e.g., Creative Commons)
- Purchase or rent from the Google Play store, Prime Video, or Netflix
- Scan only books the school has purchased and intends to use for read-aloud; photocopying for educational purposes as per the Fair Use policy (Section 52, Copyright Act)
- Cite sources taken from any online or print media after verifying usage rights
- Seek permission from the author or publisher before using drama or movie scripts for performances
- Cite all images on the same slide in presentations or under the image in documents
- If using text or any product generated by an AI tool, clearly reference the AI tool in the body and in the bibliography

**Don't:**
- Use the latest resources unavailable for legal purchase (such as recent movies)
- Download from peer-to-peer sharing networks or piracy sites (e.g., Torrent)
- Share scanned copies with parents or students through any means
- Add image links in speaker notes

## Role of the Librarian

The librarian works with Programme Coordinators and students to develop research skills aligned with this policy. In Senior School, the librarian helps teachers and students follow MLA 7 citation guidelines, displays MLA 7 posters in the library, and conducts citation sessions for teachers and students.

## Role of Programme Coordinators

- Act as role models of honest, ethical, and responsible behaviour
- Plan academic integrity sessions for all staff and students
- Update staff with new IB publications on Academic Integrity
- Ensure teachers follow the same procedures as students
- Review and communicate this policy; ensure it is available on the school website
- Empower the academic team with resources to detect malpractice
- Report suspected academic misconduct and school maladministration to IB
- Ensure MYP/DP undertakings are signed by parents and students
