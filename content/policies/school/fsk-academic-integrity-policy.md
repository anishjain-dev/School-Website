## A. Mission Alignment

Fountainhead School's mission — *To nurture leaders with character and competence* — requires that every academic achievement is earned with integrity. Academic integrity is not a rule imposed on students; it is the condition under which learning is real. This policy articulates what honest scholarship looks like at every stage of a student's journey at Fountainhead.

## B. Purpose

This policy:
- Defines academic misconduct, including the use of AI tools, plagiarism, collusion, and duplication of work
- Sets out responsibilities for students, teachers, parents, and school coordinators
- Describes the consequences of misconduct at each programme level
- Provides guidance on citation and attribution

## C. IB Learner Profile Attributes

Two attributes of the IB Learner Profile are directly activated by this policy:

- **Principled** — students act with integrity and honesty, with a strong sense of fairness and justice, and take responsibility for their own actions and their consequences.
- **Reflective** — students thoughtfully consider their own learning and experience, understanding their strengths and weaknesses to support their learning and personal development.

## D. Fountainhead Values

- **Fairness** — a student who submits another's work as their own gains an unfair advantage. This policy protects the fairness of assessments for every student.
- **Responsible** — students are responsible for their own work and for understanding where the boundary between collaboration and collusion lies.
- **Respect** — respecting the intellectual effort of authors, researchers, and peers by acknowledging their contributions through citation.
- **Integrity** — honest work, even when difficult, is the foundation of genuine competence.

## E. Connection to IB Standards

The school implements, communicates, and regularly reviews an Academic Integrity policy consistent with IB standards (Standard 0301-02). The policy is reviewed by the academic leadership team and is aligned with the IB's publication *Academic Integrity* (2019) and subsequent IB guidance on generative AI.

## F. Key Definitions

**Academic misconduct** — any behaviour that gives a student an unfair advantage or disadvantages other students, including but not limited to the acts defined below.

**Plagiarism** — presenting the ideas, words, data, images, or work of another person as one's own, whether from a human author or an AI system, without proper attribution.

**Collusion** — supporting another student's misconduct by allowing one's own work to be copied or submitted as theirs; working together on a task that requires individual effort without disclosure.

**Duplication of work** — submitting the same work, or substantially the same work, to fulfil two different assessment requirements without explicit permission from the teacher or coordinator.

**AI-generated content** — text, images, code, or other outputs produced by a generative AI tool (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or similar). Using AI-generated content and presenting it as original human work constitutes plagiarism under this policy. Transparent use of AI as a research or drafting tool, with disclosure and proper citation, is addressed at the programme level below.

## G. Citation Standard

From Grade 7 onwards, students use **MLA 7th edition** as the school's citation standard across all subject areas. Teachers introduce and model MLA citation within their subject context. The school librarian provides citation resources and guidance.

For AI tools, students are expected to cite as follows:
- Name the tool used (e.g., "ChatGPT by OpenAI, version GPT-4")
- State the date of use
- Describe the nature of the use (e.g., "used to generate an initial outline, subsequently substantially revised")

## H. Responsibilities

### Teachers
- Design assessments that promote authentic, original thinking
- Explain academic integrity expectations clearly before every significant task
- Model proper citation in their own materials
- Use plagiarism detection tools as appropriate (not as a replacement for professional judgment)
- Document and report suspected misconduct through the school's formal process
- Provide guidance on the appropriate use of AI tools within their subject

### Students
- Submit only their own work unless collaboration is explicitly authorised
- Attribute all sources accurately using the school's citation standard
- Declare the use of AI tools where they have been used, in accordance with teacher guidance
- Ask their teacher before the submission deadline if they are uncertain about what is permitted

### Parents and Guardians
- Encourage and support their child's independent work at home
- Contact the teacher if they have concerns about assessment demands or their child's stress
- Understand the school's expectations regarding tutoring: tutors support learning; they do not complete assessments

### ALS Coordinator and Librarian
- Support students in understanding citation and academic integrity practices
- Advise teachers on differentiating integrity expectations for students with Individualised Education Plans (IEPs) and language learners, without lowering expectations for honesty

## I. Programme-Specific Expectations

### Early Years Programme (EYP) — Nursery to KG

At this stage, the goal is building honest habits and the vocabulary of attribution. Children are taught:
- That stories and ideas come from people, and we name those people
- That copying someone's drawing without their permission is not kind
- The difference between their own ideas and ideas they heard from someone else

Formal consequences do not apply at this stage; the response is educational, not disciplinary.

### Primary Years Programme (PYP) — Grades 1 to 6

Students develop an understanding of intellectual ownership and the importance of acknowledging sources:
- Grade 1–3: verbal attribution ("I got this idea from…"), simple in-text acknowledgements
- Grade 4–6: introduction to bibliography; acknowledging digital and print sources; understanding why copying is unfair

The homeroom teacher handles incidents at PYP level in conversation with parents. No formal disciplinary consequence is applied for a first incident, but the incident is documented.

### Middle Years Programme (MYP) — Grades 7 to 10

MLA 7th edition citation is used from Grade 7. Expectations are explicit and taught within each subject area.

**Consequences for misconduct:**

| Incident | Consequence |
|----------|------------|
| First offence | Verbal warning; parent informed; work redone |
| Second offence | Parent meeting with subject teacher and MYP Coordinator; mark withheld pending resubmission |
| Third offence or serious misconduct | Suspension from school; formal record on student file; Principal involved |

MYP eAssessment tasks follow IB-specific procedures. Suspected misconduct in eAssessments is reported to the IB through the Programme Coordinator.

**AI use at MYP level:** Teachers specify per-task whether AI tools may be used as a research aid (with citation) or must not be used. If no instruction is given, the default is that AI-generated content must not be submitted as the student's own work.

### Diploma Programme (DP) — Grades 11 to 12

The Diploma Programme operates under IB Academic Integrity regulations, which take precedence in all matters of misconduct. The school's policy supplements — and does not contradict — IB requirements.

- The Diploma Coordinator is the first point of contact for any suspected misconduct
- Internal Assessment work is monitored by subject teachers and the Coordinator
- Extended Essay and Theory of Knowledge submissions are checked before dispatch to the IB
- Students sign an academic honesty declaration for all formal IB submissions
- Misconduct findings by the IB may result in a candidate being reported to the Final Award Committee, with consequences up to and including cancellation of the Diploma

**AI use at DP level:** The IB's current position (as updated periodically) is that AI-generated content submitted as a student's own work constitutes misconduct. Students must follow teacher guidance for each specific task. The school's Diploma Coordinator communicates IB updates on AI use to students and teachers as they are issued.

## J. Process for Reporting and Investigating Misconduct

1. Teacher or staff member identifies suspected misconduct and documents evidence
2. The matter is referred to the Programme Coordinator
3. The student is interviewed; their account is heard before any conclusion is reached
4. Parents are informed
5. The appropriate consequence is applied based on the programme level and severity
6. The incident is recorded on the student's internal file

Suspected misconduct is treated as innocent until the investigation is complete. No mark is awarded or withheld pending investigation outcome.

## K. Policy Revision

This policy is reviewed every four years or when IB guidance on academic integrity or AI tools is substantially updated. The current version was reviewed in 2024. The next review is due in 2028. Review is led by the Academic Integrity Committee, which includes the Principal, Programme Coordinators, and the school librarian.
