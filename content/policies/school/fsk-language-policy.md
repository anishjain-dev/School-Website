## A. Mission Alignment

Fountainhead School's mission — *To nurture leaders with character and competence* — is realised only when every student has full access to learning. Language is the gateway to that access. This policy ensures all students can engage with the curriculum, develop voice, and achieve their potential, regardless of the language they speak at home.

## B. School Profile

Fountainhead School Kunkni is a K–12 IB World School in Surat, Gujarat. The school offers the Primary Years Programme (PYP), Middle Years Programme (MYP), and Diploma Programme (DP).

**Community language profile:**
- 95.5% of students speak Gujarati or Hindi as their home and family language (HFL)
- 97.5% of students are second-language learners of English
- The school community also includes speakers of other Indian regional languages

## C. Languages of Instruction

| Language | Role | Grades |
|----------|------|--------|
| English | Primary language of instruction | EYP–Grade 12 |
| Hindi | Language B (second language) | Grades 1–12 |
| Gujarati | Language B (second language) | Grades 1–8 |

Students in the Diploma Programme may choose Hindi or Gujarati as a Language A or Language B option depending on their profile.

## D. IB Learner Profile Attributes

This policy is grounded in the IB Learner Profile. Communicators — who express themselves confidently in more than one language — and Open-minded learners — who appreciate their own and others' linguistic heritage — are the profile attributes most directly served by this policy.

## E. Fountainhead Values

The school's values of **Respect** and **Fairness** underpin every decision in this policy. Respecting a student's home language is not a courtesy; it is a pedagogical commitment. Fairness means no student is disadvantaged because English is not their first language.

## F. Programme-Level Language Approaches

### Early Years Programme (EYP) and Primary Years Programme (PYP)

In the early years, language development is holistic. Teachers:
- Use the home language as a bridge to English where appropriate
- Incorporate listening, speaking, reading, and writing as integrated skills
- Build sight vocabulary and phonological awareness through structured, inquiry-based tasks
- Celebrate multilingualism through classroom displays, texts, and family involvement

### Middle Years Programme (MYP)

English is the primary medium of instruction across all subject areas. Language across the curriculum (LAC) principles mean every teacher is a language teacher. MYP teachers:
- Front-load key vocabulary before introducing new concepts
- Use visual organisers, sentence frames, and graphic tools
- Assess language development alongside subject content

### Diploma Programme (DP)

Students in Grades 11–12 select language options according to their Language A and Language B choices. The school offers:
- English A: Literature and Language & Literature (Language A)
- Hindi and Gujarati (Language A or B, depending on student profile)
- English B for students who require continued language support

## G. Additional Learning Support (ALS) for Language

Many students who qualify for ALS do so because they are first-generation English learners (FGL) — students who receive zero or minimal English support at home. The ALS programme:
- Provides Intra-Grade support (within the regular grade) and Inter-Grade support (through Individualised Education Plans)
- Focuses on listening, speaking, reading, and writing as distinct but connected skills
- Uses the Response to Intervention (RTI) model: high-quality classroom instruction first, then targeted supplementary support, then intensive individual intervention

Students are referred to ALS through a process that involves the homeroom teacher, ALS coordinator, school counsellor, and parents. Referrals are reviewed twice yearly — at the start of the academic year and mid-year.

## H. Home and Family Language Programme

The school actively supports home language maintenance. This is not merely a cultural gesture; it is a cognitive and academic one. Research consistently shows that strong first-language literacy underpins second-language acquisition.

Strategies include:
- Encouraging reading in the home language alongside English
- Welcoming multilingual family participation in school events
- Providing library resources in Gujarati and Hindi

## I. Translanguaging

The school recognises translanguaging — the fluid movement between languages in a single communicative act — as a natural cognitive strategy, not a sign of deficiency. Teachers are trained to use translanguaging purposefully to explain concepts, scaffold understanding, and validate students' full linguistic repertoire.

## J. Library

The school library is a multilingual resource. The librarian:
- Maintains a collection that includes texts in English, Hindi, and Gujarati
- Curates reading recommendations that reflect students' cultural backgrounds
- Collaborates with classroom teachers on inquiry units to provide language-appropriate resources

## K. Professional Development

Teachers receive ongoing professional development in language pedagogy, including:
- Strategies for teaching English language learners (ELL)
- Approaches to Language Across the Curriculum (LAC)
- Differentiated instruction for multilingual classrooms
- ALS referral processes and IEP support

The school collaborates with IB professional development resources and external language specialists.
