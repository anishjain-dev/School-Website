## Connection to the School Mission

Fountainhead School's mission — **"To nurture leaders with character and competence"** — is at the heart of this policy. Competence is interpreted as proficiency in more than one language, and character as taking pride in one's English skills as well as one's home and family language. The language policy and school mission are therefore fully aligned.

## Objective

This document consolidates Fountainhead School's beliefs about language learning, and outlines systems and strategies to support:

- The development of English as a *lingua franca* across all programmes
- The development and maintenance of home and family languages (HFL) in the school community
- Compliance with IBO Standards and Practices as an authorised IB World School

## Languages at Fountainhead School

English is the medium of instruction across all three IB programmes — EYP, PYP, MYP, and DP. It has priority because it is the preferred teaching language expected by parents, the language of scientific and official communication in India, and the gateway to the MYP and DP curricula.

### Language Pathways by Programme

| Programme | Language A (Instruction) | Language B (Acquisition) | 3rd Language |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EYP** (Nursery – Sr. KG) | English | — | — |
| **PYP** (Grade 1–6) | English | Hindi | Gujarati (from Grade 2) |
| **MYP** (Grade 7–10) | English | Hindi / student choice | Gujarati (till Grade 8) |
| **IBDP** (Grade 11–12) | English A / Hindi A | Hindi B, English B, French ab initio | — |

Students whose best language is not English may opt for a school-supported self-taught subject in Group 1 or Group 2 in the DP. Spanish ab initio and Mandarin ab initio are additionally available through Pamoja Education.

## Our Beliefs about Language and Language Learning

**Language is fundamental to learning, thinking, and communicating.** Language connects us to others, helps us understand the world, and is the tool through which meaning is constructed. Learners acquire language by first listening, then speaking, then reading, then writing — with these strands always interwoven.

**Language is a vehicle for inquiry.** Cognitive Academic Language Proficiency (CALP) is essential for all learners to participate fully in IB programmes. The school's teaching is learner-centred, contextual, and cognitively rich.

**Language affirms identity and develops international-mindedness.** The school recognises that the majority of students are additional language learners of English. It plays a pivotal role in providing an enriching environment that empowers every learner to participate fully in academics while affirming their multilingual identity.

**Multilingualism benefits the whole learning community.** It enriches personal development, develops cognitive skills around attention and problem-solving, and deepens understanding of diverse sociocultural contexts. The school actively promotes additive multilingualism — English as an addition to, not a replacement for, home and family languages.

## Language Profile of Students

Fountainhead School, Surat draws students primarily from upper-middle-class and affluent families — entrepreneurs, doctors, finance and technology professionals. As of 2023:

- **95.5%** of students have Gujarati or Hindi as their home/family language
- **97.5%** are second-language learners of English
- **16.3%** are first-generation learners of English
- **98.1%** of parents prefer English as the medium of study for their child

## Home and Family Language (HFL) Programme

Hindi and Gujarati are the HFLs of 95.5% of the student population. The school teaches both: Hindi from Grade 1 to Grade 12; Gujarati from Grade 1 to Grade 8 (also mandated by the Gujarat state government).

Strategies to support HFL development school-wide include:

- A bilingual library collection including books, periodicals, and folk tales in Hindi, Gujarati, and other regional languages
- Hindi Bhasha Divas and Gujarati Bhasha Divas celebrations across all programmes — games, quizzes, skits, assemblies
- Assemblies on International Mindedness that showcase regional languages through music, dance, and drama
- Parents are encouraged to maintain HFL at home through reading, music, and daily conversation
- In Senior School, mother-tongue assemblies where students share stories, plays, movies, and songs in their first language

## Translanguaging

Fountainhead views translanguaging as a communication strategy, not a pedagogical compromise. HFLs can be used as a support language wherever the teacher sees value — for example, activating prior knowledge, recording Early Years anecdotes verbatim, or exploring cultural beliefs. The guideline is: *HFLs support but do not substitute* English language development.

Teachers use translanguaging in planning to consider:

- Language backgrounds and needs of each student
- Whether to scaffold content, language, or both
- Which unit aspects are best approached through home language (cultural knowledge, local context)

## Additional Learning Support (ALS)

Almost all students at Fountainhead are second-language learners of English. The school does not run a separate ESL/EAL programme — it views all students at entry age as language learners. In the Early Years, listening and speaking exercises take priority.

Students who need additional support include those who are first-generation learners of English, come from schools where English is not the medium of instruction, or are admitted to a higher grade with poor English foundations. ALS consists of classroom support and/or pull-out sessions for a stipulated time. By MYP, English is typically the best language for most students.

## Role of the Library

The library is central to the language programme. It is resourced with fiction, non-fiction, picture books, chapter books, multilingual books, world classics, autobiographies, reference books, and encyclopaedias accessible to all students and staff. The library team runs author week, poetry month, and author birthday celebrations to promote reading as a school culture.
