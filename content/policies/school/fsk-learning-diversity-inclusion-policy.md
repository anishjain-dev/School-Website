## Connection to the School Mission

**"To nurture leaders with character and competence"** — Fountainhead School's mission is grounded in the belief that every student is a potential leader. Inclusive education is not an add-on; it is how the mission is realised. The school identifies and addresses each student's unique learning needs, fosters their strengths, and creates a nurturing, empowering environment that prepares every learner to thrive.

## School Vision on Inclusion

At Fountainhead School, Inclusive Education is an ongoing process designed to enhance access and engagement for all students by identifying and overcoming barriers to learning. This is rooted in a culture of collaboration, mutual respect, support, and effective problem-solving.

The school is guided by the IB's four principles of good inclusive practice:

1. **Affirming identity and building self-esteem**
2. **Valuing prior knowledge**
3. **Scaffolding**
4. **Extending learning**

## Practices and Procedures to Remove Barriers to Learning

### School Organisation

- Class size maintained at 20 students to enable differentiated attention
- Smaller learning groups supported by ALS teachers for Grades 1–5
- Curriculum regularly reviewed to include diverse perspectives and meet the needs of all learners
- School-wide development processes support a culture of diversity and equity

### Approaches to Teaching and Learning

- Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles applied in lesson design
- Differentiated instruction — tasks matched to interest, readiness, and proficiency level
- Diverse learning experiences that accommodate varied learning styles
- Access arrangements and reasonable adjustments consistently applied

### Community Relationships

- Daily positive interactions that foster a supportive environment
- Open communication and active listening so every student and family feels valued
- IB Learner Profile embedded in school culture to empower student agency and identity

## Additional Learning Support (ALS) — Primary Years Programme

### Who Can Qualify

A student may be considered for ALS if they:

- Are learning English as a first-generation or non-native speaker
- Face challenges understanding classroom instructions or grade-level texts
- Do not meet expected language proficiency for their previous grade
- Receive minimal English support at home
- Have been diagnosed with a condition that affects learning
- Require extra support in mathematics to reach grade-level standards

### Identification Process

The school uses the **Response to Intervention (RTI)** model — a multi-tiered approach for early identification. Identification involves:

- Pre-assessment data, listening, speaking, and reading assessments
- Class observations and formal/informal testing
- Parental involvement and review of prior IEPs (if applicable)
- Input from the ALS coordinator, homeroom teachers, counselors, and team leaders

This process runs formally twice a year: at the beginning and in the middle of the academic year.

### ALS Roles

- **Intra-Grade ALS** — supports students with literacy, numeracy, and general learning needs within their own grade
- **Inter-Grade ALS** — focuses on language development and students with Individualised Education Plans (IEPs)

### Access Arrangements Provided

- Extra time for task completion and assessments
- Larger font materials and graphic organisers where needed
- Word bank sheets and explicit oral instructions
- Calculator use for mathematics tasks and assessments
- Verbal expression in place of written work for students with mild dysgraphia
- Modified expectations and simplified language as appropriate

### Individualised Education Plans (IEPs)

An IEP is created for students with disabilities or first-generation English learners (FGL) to ensure personalised educational services. The IEP process involves:

1. Identifying the need
2. Meeting with parents and teachers to set goals
3. Documenting strategies and details
4. Regular observations and updates
5. Quarterly summaries before parent meetings

## Addressing Learning Diversity — Middle Years and Diploma Programme

### Admission Criteria

Students whose abilities do not affect their capacity to meet programme demands are admitted. This is assessed through psychological reports from qualified professionals and the school's academic requirements.

### Strategies for Meeting Student Needs

- Focus on strength areas to build confidence and motivation
- Active learning strategies for first-hand experience
- Providing extra time for assignments and assessments
- Using clear, accessible language — starting with familiar languages where needed
- Differentiated activities matched to levels of attainment

### Access Arrangements for Assessments

The school wellbeing counsellor monitors and coordinates access arrangements in partnership with parents, teachers, and programme coordinators. Arrangements include extra time, use of a word processor/reader/writer, and other accommodations as prescribed by IB Access and Inclusion policy. For final IB exams, parent consent is collected and the arrangement request is submitted to the IB by the Programme Coordinator.

## Responsibilities

| Stakeholder | Key Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **ALS Coordinator** | Training teachers, collecting diversity data, maintaining IEPs, open communication with families |
| **School Counselor** | Counselling interventions, contributing to IEP development, anti-bullying, parent guidance |
| **Teacher** | UDL lesson design, ongoing formative data, open parent communication, inclusive classroom culture |
| **Student** | Advocating for their own needs, showing respect for peers, reporting bullying or exclusion |
| **Parent** | Regular communication with teachers, attending IEP meetings, supporting accommodations at home |

## Professional Development

Teachers receive training through new-hire induction, on-the-job learning, and external professional development. Fountainhead School is closely associated with the **Gateway School of Mumbai** for inclusive education guidance. INSET sessions led by school counsellors prepare teachers to screen and support students with diverse needs.
