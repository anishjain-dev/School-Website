## A. Mission Alignment

Fountainhead School's mission — *To nurture leaders with character and competence* — applies to every student, without exception. This policy gives that commitment operational substance: it describes how the school identifies students with diverse learning needs, what support it provides, and what it expects from every member of the school community in return.

## B. School Vision on Inclusion

Inclusive Education at Fountainhead is an ongoing process, not a fixed state. The school aims to enhance access and engagement for all students by identifying and overcoming barriers to learning. This is achieved through a culture of collaboration, mutual respect, and problem-solving.

The school aligns its inclusive practice with the IB's four principles of good practice:
1. Affirming identity and building self-esteem
2. Valuing prior knowledge
3. Scaffolding
4. Extending learning

## C. IB Learner Profile Attributes

**Caring** — the school community prioritises the well-being of each student and actively works to remove barriers to learning for those facing challenges.

**Open-minded** — the school embraces diverse perspectives and adapts teaching methods to accommodate various learning styles.

**Reflective** — teaching methods, curriculum design, and student interactions are regularly assessed for their effectiveness with diverse learners.

## D. Fountainhead Values

**Learner** — every student's unique learning journey is valid. Education is tailored to accommodate diverse learning preferences, abilities, and backgrounds.

**Respect** — every individual is treated with dignity. Discrimination and bias are actively discouraged.

**Fairness** — equitable access to learning opportunities is a right, not a favour.

## E. Connection to IB Standards

This policy is aligned with IB Standards and Practices:
- **0202-02:** The school identifies and provides appropriate learning support
- **0301-01:** The school secures access to IB education for the broadest possible range of students
- **0301-02:** The school implements, communicates, and regularly reviews an inclusion policy
- **0403-05:** Teachers remove barriers to learning to enable all students to achieve their goals
- **0404-03:** Assessments are administered consistently, fairly, inclusively, and transparently

## F. Practices to Remove Barriers to Learning

### School Organisation and Resources

- Class sizes are maintained at a maximum of 20 students in Grades 1–5, with ALS pull-out groups operating in addition to classroom instruction
- The school funds an ALS Coordinator and ALS teachers as dedicated inclusion staff
- Curriculum is regularly reviewed to include diverse perspectives and address the needs of all students
- Teachers receive ongoing training in inclusive practice

### Culture and Policies

- All staff are expected to adhere to the school's diversity and equity commitments
- Open dialogue about diverse learning needs is encouraged among staff, students, and families
- A growth mindset approach is embedded in professional development

### Approaches to Teaching and Learning

- Differentiated instruction is standard practice, not exceptional accommodation
- Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles guide lesson planning
- Access arrangements are consistently and fairly applied across all subjects

### Physical Environment

- School facilities are accessible to all community members
- Classroom layouts are arranged flexibly to support varied learning modes

### Community Relationships

- Parents are partners in their child's learning; regular communication and involvement are expected
- The school community participates in decisions about inclusion and belonging

## G. Additional Learning Support (ALS) — Primary Years Programme

### G.1 Objective of the ALS Programme

The ALS programme supports students across a wide range of learning challenges, including:
- ADHD and attention difficulties
- Communication, social, and emotional needs
- Behavioural challenges
- First-generation learners of English (FGL)
- Students requiring academic assistance not available within the regular classroom

**ALS roles:**
- **Intra-Grade ALS:** supports literacy, numeracy, and other learning needs within the student's regular grade group
- **Inter-Grade ALS:** focuses on language development and works with students on Individualised Education Plans (IEPs)

### G.2 Who Can Qualify for ALS

A student may be considered for ALS if they meet one or more of the following criteria:
- Learning English as a first-generation or non-native speaker
- Unable to understand classroom instructions or grade-level texts
- Not meeting expected language proficiency for their previous grade
- Receiving minimal English support at home
- Diagnosed with a condition affecting the learning process
- Has needs that consistently affect their own or others' learning
- Requires extra help in mathematics to reach grade-level standards
- Needs in-class assistance to stay on track academically

### G.3 Identifying Students for ALS

The school uses the **Response to Intervention (RTI)** model — a multi-tiered approach to early identification and support. Key components include high-quality classroom instruction, ongoing assessment, tiered and explicit instruction, and parent involvement.

Selection criteria include:
- Pre-assessment data
- Listening, speaking, and reading assessments
- Classroom observations
- Formal or informal testing reports
- Parental input
- Review of existing IEP (if applicable)
- Consultation with the school counsellor

This process is conducted formally twice yearly — at the start of the academic year and mid-year. Those involved include the student, ALS teachers, team leaders, homeroom teachers, counsellors, parents, and the ALS Coordinator.

### G.4 Access Arrangements for ALS Students

Students receiving ALS support may be provided with:
- Larger font size on tasks and assessment worksheets
- Reading aloud of questions by the ALS teacher where needed
- Graphic organisers for students who struggle with organising written ideas
- A word bank sheet during assessments to support vocabulary recall
- Permission to supplement written work with verbal expression (for students with mild dysgraphia)
- Use of a calculator for mathematics tasks and assessments where the calculation itself is not the learning objective

### G.5 Individualised Education Plan (IEP)

An IEP is created for students with disabilities or identified as first-generation English learners who require a structured, documented support plan. The IEP:
- Tailors education to the individual student's needs
- Identifies necessary accommodations
- Sets goals and tracks progress
- Specifies teaching strategies
- Records meeting details and relevant family information

**IEP process:**
1. Identify the need for an IEP
2. Meet with parents and teachers to set goals
3. Document strategies and details
4. Conduct regular observations and updates
5. Prepare quarterly summaries before parent meetings

## H. Responsibilities of Stakeholders

### H.1 ALS Coordinator
- Organises ongoing teacher training on diversity and inclusion
- Collects and analyses student performance data to identify and address gaps
- Facilitates teamwork among teachers, administrators, and support staff
- Ensures resources for diverse learners are maintained
- Evaluates the effectiveness of inclusive practices
- Maintains open communication with parents
- Ensures student information confidentiality
- Supports the development and implementation of IEPs
- Guides parents on supporting their child's learning at home

### H.2 School Counsellor
- Provides counselling for behavioural, social, and emotional concerns
- Contributes to IEP development alongside ALS teachers and Special Education Consultants
- Addresses bullying, discrimination, and social exclusion
- Works with parents to support children with unique learning needs
- Collects data on student well-being and tracks intervention impact
- Organises awareness workshops and campaigns

### H.3 Teachers
- Modify teaching methods and assessments to meet diverse learning needs
- Foster inclusive classrooms where bullying and discrimination are addressed
- Work with ALS teachers to identify students with learning needs
- Design lessons using UDL principles
- Maintain open communication with parents
- Participate in ongoing inclusive-practice training

### H.4 Students
- Respect and value diversity; oppose bullying and discrimination
- Show kindness to peers, particularly those with different needs
- Speak up for their own needs and those of peers
- Provide constructive feedback to help improve the learning environment
- Report bullying, discrimination, or exclusion

### H.5 Parents
- Maintain regular communication with teachers and counsellors
- Engage with the school's learning diversity and inclusion policies
- Attend parent-teacher conferences and IEP meetings
- Support accommodations made for their child and others
- Demonstrate inclusive behaviour at home and in the community

## I. Middle Years and Diploma Programme — Senior School

### I.1 Admission Criteria for Students with Different Educational Needs

The school will admit a student with diverse educational needs if their abilities do not prevent them from meeting the demands of the MYP or DP programme. This is assessed through:
- Psychological reports from qualified specialists
- School academic requirements review

Additional conditions for admission:
- An appropriate supporting teacher can be identified (by the school or the parent)
- Parents are willing to meet the costs of any specialist support required

### I.2 Approaches and Strategies — Senior School

Teachers focus on:
- Building on students' key strength areas to develop confidence and motivation
- Providing special assistance to help students move beyond their learning challenges
- Using active learning strategies for first-hand experience
- Differentiating activities to ensure all learners are engaged
- Matching expectations to the level of attainment
- Using clear, accessible language — including the student's mother tongue where appropriate as a bridging tool
- Providing extra time for class assignments and assessments

### I.3 Access Arrangements for Assessments

- Early identification is prioritised; the school wellbeing counsellor liaises with parents as soon as concerns arise
- A teacher checklist (Appendix 1) supports early identification of students needing counsellor intervention
- Access arrangements may include: extra time, use of a word processor, reader, writer, or other adjustments prescribed by IB Access and Inclusion policy
- The Senior School Principal/Programme Coordinator and Assessment Coordinator (MYP) are part of the decision-making process
- Decisions consider the student's past performance, educational evidence, and medical/psychological reports
- Parents who do not wish to use inclusive access arrangements must confirm this in writing to the school

**For Final IB Exams:**
- Parent consent is obtained for each application of access arrangement (Appendix 2)
- Educational evidence is provided by the subject teacher
- A psychologist's report is required and kept on file
- The Programme Coordinator submits the Inclusive Access Arrangement request to the IB
- Once IB approval is received, students and parents are informed
- Students with colour blindness are identified separately; medical reports are obtained and arrangements made
- IB "Access and Inclusion Policy" (September 2022) is followed in all cases
- In adverse circumstances during examinations, IB Adverse Circumstances procedures apply

## J. Remediation and Therapy Outside School

On a case-by-case basis, the school may recommend additional support outside school in the form of remediation or therapy. The recommendation is made in consultation with the ALS Coordinator, counsellor, and relevant teachers.

## K. Grade Repetition

The school may recommend that a student repeat a grade if:
- The student continues to make little to no progress in identified areas of concern
- The student continues to have significant difficulty acquiring literacy and numeracy skills

Parental agreement is required before grade repetition takes place.

## L. Conditions that May Affect Continued Enrolment

At any point, the school may reconsider a student's admission if one or more of the following conditions are present:
- Emotional, behavioural, or social needs that regularly and significantly affect the learning of the student or others
- Sensory or physical needs requiring intensive specialist equipment or regular specialist visits that the school cannot provide
- Parental support that is not sufficient to meet the school's reasonable expectations, particularly if this is actively hampering the student's growth
- The school is unable to add reasonable educational value to the student, whatever the cause

The school is the final authority in making decisions about continued enrolment under these conditions.

## M. Professional Development

Teachers attend workshops to build skills in inclusion and diverse-learner support. The school has a formal relationship with the **Gateway School of Mumbai** for guidance and training in supporting students with learning challenges. INSET sessions led by school counsellors prepare teachers to screen students and respond appropriately.

## N. Sensitisation of Teachers

School workshops are conducted to sensitise teachers to the range of behaviours they may encounter and how to respond constructively. Regular meetings are held between the mentor teacher, subject teachers, counsellors, and ALS teachers to ensure coordination between in-class teaching and pull-out support.

## O. Sensitisation of Students

Students are educated about the needs of differently abled peers and the importance of inclusive attitudes. Sensitisation sessions are held in classrooms at regular intervals, led by homeroom teachers, mentor teachers, ALS teachers, or counsellors, as appropriate.

## P. Connection with Other School Policies

- **Language Policy** — many ALS students are first-generation English learners; the Language Policy and the ALS programme are closely coordinated
- **Assessment Policy** — diverse learning needs are monitored against personal and academic goals; evidence of learning is compiled and reported to all relevant stakeholders

## Q. Policy Revision

This policy is reviewed every four years, aligned with the latest IB Inclusion and Access policy. The current policy was reviewed in 2024. The next review is due in May 2026. The review committee is led by a member of the academic leadership team and includes representation from the school community.
